# Casebook and transfer limits

These examples are method evidence, not templates whose numeric thresholds or outcomes may be copied into a new assembly.

## Orbita full assembly baseline

The original governed run connected 94 controlled process steps to 167 rigid CAD occurrences and collapsed them into 97 solver units. It exposed purchased-module granularity, class-scoped direction rules, flexible/no-geometry omissions, tool-grip differences and the need to report path coverage rather than a single success rate. Cross-checks agreed on direction for 97/97 units, while 10 distance discrepancies remained explicitly unresolved. Transfer lesson: preserve occurrence-to-solver-unit provenance and never hide unresolved cross-tool differences.

## Orbita B13 successor/closed-chain case

B13 established 33/33 unique occurrence identities, paired six joint axes and six screw ports through two independent implementations, and corrected five old heuristic assignments. A closed-chain interference invariant also disproved an initial rigid-lock assumption. Transfer lesson: exact identity, interface topology and mechanism semantics must precede generic motion search.

## Orbita B14 bounded physical probe

B14 compared multiple SE(3) direction candidates and produced historically
distinguishable response signatures, but the D2 label remained provisional:
the later fair-comparison audit found that not every candidate shared a closed
ten-facet comparison contract. Physical release also remained blocked. Its
environment included open-surface geometry and intentionally rigidified known
joints. Transfer lesson: a useful response signature can still be unfit even
for direction ranking; disclose proxy geometry, frozen degrees of freedom and
unrun candidates, and never copy a case label as a target verdict.

## 灵犀 X1 cross-project transfer

X1 tested the ontology at a different scale and naming regime: 1,558 occurrences, 187 definitions, 15,094 local cylinder faces, 31,586 instantiated cylinder faces and 2,783 occurrence interface pairs. Independent STEP and XCAF chains first disagreed on every leaf pose, revealing a reversed transform interpretation; after correction they agreed 1,558/1,558. The project also exposed raw GB18030 names, normalization/key collisions, six surface-only definitions, scale-dependent meshing, missing planar/conical ports and sequence-aware collision failures. A first path implementation let 146 of 352 playable movers acquire non-baseline contact; the corrected interval check left 307 certified playable paths and 45 honestly unplayable items. Transfer lesson: plausible outputs are not validation—independent transform checks, deterministic key joins and full-interval collision evidence are mandatory.

Later X1 animation iterations added three transferable controls: test material
containment in both directions so a large mover cannot swallow a small
fastener unnoticed; derive fastener presence timing from the connection graph;
and retain every blocking occurrence in a physical-constraint ledger instead
of silently trimming or hiding it. The saved animation remained a visual
baseline with explicit motion and review holds, not physical release.

## Poppy native-completeness case

Poppy separated a visually convincing main structure from installed-content
completeness. The native Pack-and-Go and STEP trees could be reconciled, yet the
native package contained only a small subset of the screws and no nuts or
harnesses required by controlled records. A later evidence-grounded queue
authorized 74 distinct display-proxy occurrences (62 nuts, eight horn screws
and four board screws) while retaining hundreds of unresolved requirements and
withholding supplier-exact, tool, sweep, interference and manufacturing
claims. Transfer lesson: procurement packs are not installed counts; every
hardware slot needs a distinct occurrence, exact placement evidence and an
explicit geometry/claim class. A create-only proxy queue is progress, not a
complete native assembly.

## Fourier N1 source-native tree recovery

Fourier N1 recovered a controlled native closure of 210 external files and a
saved tree of 3,924 expanded occurrences. Five virtual roots were recoverable
as metadata subgraphs but not standalone exact B-Rep; one non-virtual part
remained missing; all 33 harness definitions lacked exact native routed
occurrences; and a partial Fusion translation differed from the source counts
without an occurrence-level explanation. Transfer lesson: file closure,
definition recovery, expanded occurrence closure, exact geometry, BOM binding,
harness routing and source-to-Fusion reconciliation are separate claims. A
same-name STEP candidate must not silently replace a missing native part.

## Broader CAD practice examples

CAD-agent tutorial cases such as a minifigure assembly and a parameterized box with a purchased McMaster STEP demonstrate reusable component identity, provenance and native-CAD validation. They are useful supporting examples but did not execute the complete S0–S11 process lane, so do not count them as full assembly-ontology validations.

## Synthetic semantic fixtures

Positive, negative, ambiguous and prior-sensitive reverse-assembly fixtures can test reasoning and fail-closed behavior. They are regression fixtures, not real CAD or shop-floor evidence. Keep synthetic evidence in a separate namespace and never use it to validate a geometric threshold.

## Y-Zipper full-contact physics case

The contact-driven-strip case established a reusable separation between Fusion
pose authority, libuipc contact physics and Blender read-only expression. Its
strongest bounded result was contact-transfer/passage evidence under a pinned
case contract, with counterfactual controls and recovered force/torque
telemetry. It did not establish a universal friction/material profile, motor
capacity, mechanical relock or physical release. Transfer lesson: reuse the
staging, causality controls and claim ledger; mark every numeric parameter from
that case `CASE_LOCKED_EXAMPLE` until the new mechanism independently earns it.

## Paper-algorithm benchmark integrity regression

A historical algorithm-comparison JSON later drifted from its own declared
canonical hash because release-related booleans changed. The old project record
is intentionally not repaired by this skill. It is retained as a negative
regression: canonical-hash drift rejects admission, and release authority must
live in an explicit claim ledger rather than ambiguous booleans inside a paper
benchmark.

## Poppy/Fourier occurrence-film rebuild (first X1+ ACCEPTED delivery)

The 2026-08-29/30 rebuild replaced three rejected group-motion films with a
per-occurrence film that passed the X1+ acceptance contract 12/12 gates
(ACCEPTED, zero failures) plus owner review. The decisive semantics were
process-truth-shaped, not render-shaped: a predecessors DAG giving each
operation a bench scene (modular assembly — other units are absent while a
unit is built), presence-only join chapters with explicit motion HOLDs,
not-animated parts appearing only after their chapter's movers seat, a loose-gap
plane graph used strictly as an insertion-axis candidate source, and S5
solver-unit folding at the SOP's own install granularity (actuators, bearings
with modeled balls, PCBAs, battery packs). Coordinate semantics were measured
(grid clustering plus named L/R anchors), never assumed; the thick-end direction
heuristic failed domain validation on Poppy and was demoted rather than reused.
Transfer lesson: the walk in the true bench remaining set is the motion
authority; audits feed trims back into the playable table; the validator runs
before any MP4 exists. Full method delta and the Windows/SolidWorks unattended
conversion playbook: `occurrence-film-rebuild-playbook.md`.

## What is actually portable

Portable: stage order, evidence structure, identity/occurrence separation, independent transform checking, solver-unit formation, claim boundaries, fail-closed statuses, exact/proxy labelling and the requirement to certify a complete interval.

Not automatically portable: numeric tolerances, name-normalization rules, material/contact parameters, tool envelopes, flexible-part behavior, fixture frames, operation order, release distance and any project verdict. Recalibrate or revalidate these on the target assembly.
